I don't naturally wake up grateful.

Most mornings start with a mental checklist: what's on the calendar, who needs what, what we forgot to move from the freezer, what we're already behind on. Coffee usually shows up long before gratitude does. Maybe you understand.

We hear a lot about thankfulness, especially this time of year. Be grateful. Count your blessings. Remember how good you have it. And while those words might be true, they can also feel like pressure when your heart is tired. Gratitude starts to sound like one more thing you're supposed to "do better."

But what if gratitude wasn't a performance or a personality trait? What if it was simply a way of seeing?

For a long time, I thought gratitude had to be big and emotional, deeply moved by every sunrise, every answered prayer, every perfect moment. Real life doesn't always feel like that. Some days look more like dishes in the sink, unanswered texts, a body that feels worn out, and a heart that's doing its best just to keep going. On those days, it can feel almost dishonest to say, "I'm so thankful!" with a big smile.

Instead of forcing a feeling, what if we started smaller?

Gratitude in small things might look like noticing the way morning light falls across the table, the warmth of a mug in your hands, the sound of a dog's tail thumping when you walk into the room, or simply realizing your lungs are still quietly pulling in air even when you're overwhelmed. None of those moments are dramatic. They don't erase the hard things. But they can remind us that, right in the middle of what feels ordinary or heavy, God is still here.

Scripture doesn't say, "Give thanks for all circumstances." It invites us to "give thanks in all circumstances" (1 Thessalonians 5:18). That little word in matters. We're not asked to be grateful for everything. We're invited to begin noticing grace within whatever the day holds.

There are also seasons when even small gratitude feels far away. You might know those days, when prayers feel flat, joy feels distant, and other people's "thankful posts" just make you feel behind. If that's you, you're not broken and you're not failing at faith. Sometimes the most honest prayer we can offer is, "God, I want to be thankful, but I don't feel it right now. Please stay close anyway." That, too, is a kind of gratitude. It's a quiet way of saying, I still believe You're here.

So this week, you don't need a long gratitude list. Just choose one ordinary moment, pouring coffee, starting the car, turning out the light at night. Pause for a breath longer than usual and simply whisper, "Thank You for this, right here." That's it. One small noticing. One small prayer.

Over time, those little moments begin to add up, not as pressure, but as gentle reminders that you are being held in ways you don't always see.
