I never dreamed this would be my yard.

When we bought the house, I imagined myself outside every evening, hands in the dirt, carefully tending the beds and pulling the weeds before they took over. I grew up watching women who seemed to know instinctively how to coax flowers into bloom and vegetables into bounty. Surely, I thought, I would do the same. Surely I would be a gardener.

But somewhere between raising children, managing a household, and keeping up with life, the garden started running itself. A sprinkler on a timer did most of the work. Plants grew in ways I didn't plan. The weeds didn't always get pulled, and the flower beds developed a mind of their own.

One morning, as I stood at the edge of the yard sipping coffee, I looked over the mess of tangled vines and unruly blooms and realized something: I kind of liked it this way. It was wild. It was wooly. And it was beautiful.

In that moment, I laughed at myself and thought, Maybe I like my gardens the way I like my men, wild and wooly. The truth is, the garden had become a metaphor for the rest of my life. Try as I might, I could never keep everything neat and tidy. There were always unexpected twists, things that didn't go according to plan, moments that got away from me. And yet, even in the untended places, beauty managed to break through.

I'm learning that God doesn't wait for our lives to be perfectly manicured before He meets us there. Sometimes He shows up most clearly in the overgrown corners, the places where we stopped trying to control everything and just let life happen.
